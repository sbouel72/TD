# The Supply Problem

**Alternate titles**
- "Domestic Supply of Infants": The Footnote That Confessed the Whole System
- The Right to Parent Was Never Part of the Deal

**Subtitle:** A movement that says it protects children depended on a steady supply of them. Adoption is where control of women's bodies, the economics of poverty and the rescue story converge, and the child carries the cost for life.

**Tags:** adoption, reproductive justice, Dobbs, poverty, family separation

---

On 24 June 2022 the Supreme Court of the United States overturned *Roe v. Wade*. One footnote in the majority opinion in *Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization* explains adoption better than any agency brochure has. It quotes a federal health report: nearly a million women were seeking to adopt, while "the domestic supply of infants relinquished at birth or within the first month of life and available to be adopted had become virtually nonexistent."

*Supply.*

The Court was not being careless. It was answering an argument that legal abortion had become less necessary because women who did not want to raise a child had another option. The other option was surrender. The evidence that this option worked was a market: buyers waiting, product scarce.

I read that footnote and something low in my body recognised it before my mind did. It is the hum that sits under everything. It knows what it was before anyone wrote a sentence about it. It was supply.

This essay sets out an argument that Thoughtless Delineation has been building across the canon. Adoption, as a system, is not the gentle middle ground between abortion and parenthood. It is the place where three forces converge: the control of reproduction, the extraction of poverty, and a rescue narrative that turns a child's erasure into someone else's redemption. Together they make adoption more than a private harm. It is a disgrace to a species that claims to value where it came from, and a threat to the conditions under which human beings come to know themselves.

## The loop, stated plainly

The meta-framework of this publication is the Analog Fractal Feedback Recursion of Adoption. Its circuit runs: **foreclosure, silence, symptom, misattribution, control, transmission**, and then back to foreclosure one generation down, in a slightly drifted form.

Hold that circuit in mind, because each section below is a photograph of one point on it. Reproductive control forecloses the mother's options. Poverty supplies the pressure. The rescue narrative supplies the silence. The adoptee's distress becomes the symptom, which the system misattributes to the adoptee rather than to the severance. Then the system uses that symptom to justify more control. The loop needs no villain. It runs on paperwork.

## The third right

In 1994 a group of Black women in the United States named a framework that the mainstream "choice" movement had left out. They called it **reproductive justice**, and it rests on three rights: the right not to have a child, the right to have a child, and the right to parent the children you have in safe and sustainable communities.

That third right is the one adoption violates, and it is the one almost nobody defends.

The abortion debate is fought over the first two. One side defends the right not to have a child. The other side claims to defend the child's right to be born. Adoption is offered to both sides as a compromise, and the compromise is paid for entirely out of the third right. The mother keeps her pregnancy and loses her child. The child keeps their life and loses their mother, their name, their lineage and their original birth record. Everyone at the negotiating table calls this a solution, because nobody at the table is the person who was severed.

This is **Non-Adoptee Bias** at its most structural. The terms of the reproductive debate were set by people for whom the mother-child bond was never at risk. They argue about whether a child should exist. They rarely ask whether a child, once born, has a right to the people they were born to.

## Surrender is a financial event

The rescue story says that a natural mother chooses adoption out of love. The record says something else.

Across the historical and contemporary research, the conditions that produce surrender are overwhelmingly material: no money, no housing, no partner, no family support, no paid leave, no childcare. In her 2024 study *Relinquished*, the sociologist Gretchen Sisson interviewed mothers who surrendered infants in the modern era of "open" and "choice-based" adoption. Economic precarity ran through their accounts. These were not women who did not want their children. They were women who were told, in a thousand practical ways, that they could not afford them.

When a society lets poverty decide who gets to raise their own child, adoption is not charity. It is a transfer of children from people with less money to people with more. That is the **Malthusian Extraction Engine** operating within a single country rather than across borders. The receiving family's capital meets the source family's crisis at the moment of greatest leverage, and the crisis becomes a placement.

The private system makes the money visible. Domestic infant adoption in the United States routinely costs tens of thousands of dollars in agency, legal and "matching" fees. The **Maintenance Economy** names who is paid to keep the pipeline running. Notice what that money could do if it went the other way. The fee for one infant would cover a year of rent, childcare and food for the mother who surrendered under financial pressure. The system will spend on the transfer what it refuses to spend on the family.

The state system does the same thing more quietly. In child protection, "neglect" is the most common basis for removing a child, and neglect is very often poverty described as a parenting failure: no food in the fridge, unstable housing, a parent working two jobs with no one to watch the children. The family is investigated for being poor, the child is removed, and the clock starts. In the United States the Adoption and Safe Families Act of 1997 pushes states toward terminating parental rights when a child has been in care for fifteen of the most recent twenty-two months. Poverty starts the case. A statutory timeline ends the family. At no point does anyone break a rule. That is **Administrative Violence**: harm carried out by systems working exactly as designed.

## The pro-life contradiction

The movement that calls itself pro-life has a coherence problem, and adoption is where it shows.

If the principle were the protection of children, it would follow the child past birth. It would fund the mother, the household and the bond. The policy record runs the other way. The United States has no national paid parental leave. The expanded Child Tax Credit, which cut child poverty to a record low in 2021, was allowed to expire, and the Census Bureau's supplemental measure showed child poverty more than doubling the following year. Several of the states with the most restrictive abortion laws have also refused to expand Medicaid. Pregnancy is protected by law. The family that pregnancy produces is left to the market.

Adoption closes the gap in the argument. It allows a movement to force a pregnancy to term without taking responsibility for the child who follows, because someone else will take the child. At oral argument in *Dobbs*, safe haven laws, which let a parent leave a newborn at a hospital or fire station with no questions asked, were raised from the bench as a way to relieve the burden of parenthood. That is the logic exposed: the burden of the pregnancy is mandatory, and the bond can be dropped off.

Look at what the movement's own infrastructure protects. In the United States, pregnancy resource centres, many of them religiously affiliated, now outnumber abortion clinics. Their stated purpose is to keep pregnancies going, and adoption is one of the outcomes they promote. The protected object is the birth. The mother is counselled toward surrender, and the child becomes the answer to someone else's longing.

This publication has a name for that last move. **Borrowed Consciousness** describes how the adoption industry, in its evangelical "orphan theology" form and its secular rescue form alike, manufactures a moral awakening for the adopter out of the adoptee's erasure. The adopter is redeemed. The movement is vindicated. The child pays with an origin they were never allowed to keep. A movement that cared about life would not need a child's lineage as the price of its own righteousness.

## The paper lineage

Then the state finishes the job.

In most adoptions the original birth certificate is sealed or cancelled, and a new one is issued naming the adoptive parents as though they were the natural parents. The **Ontological Closed Loop** describes the result. The only authority that can confirm who the adoptee is happens to be the authority that rewrote the record. **Ego Extermino** names the act underneath: *I exterminate your past, therefore I am your parent.* Adoption culture then installs that act as a debt of gratitude, so that the loss is never allowed to be called a loss.

This is not history. Many jurisdictions still restrict adult adoptees' access to their own original records. In 2013 Australia's national government formally apologised for forced adoption practices after a 2012 Senate inquiry that counted up to 250,000 adoptions since 1940. The apology was real. The records system, the training system and the redress system it implied were never fully built. Saying sorry and then leaving the machinery running is **Bureaucratic Absorption**: the testimony is taken, and the structure stays as it was.

## Why this is a species-level problem

The claim that adoption threatens human development will sound like an exaggeration to anyone who thinks of adoption as a private family arrangement. It stops sounding like one when you look at what the system teaches a society to accept.

**Epistemicide** is the destruction of a group's ways of knowing itself. Each sealed record is a small instance, slow and low in intensity, which is why it never appears on any ledger of harms. Multiply it across the forced-adoption eras, the Stolen Generations in Australia, the Indian Adoption Project and the removals that made the Indian Child Welfare Act necessary in the United States, and the intercountry pipelines out of South Korea, Guatemala, Chile and elsewhere. South Korea's Truth and Reconciliation Commission found in 2025 that the state bore responsibility for human rights violations in its overseas adoption program. What you see is not a series of scandals but a single method: separate the child from the source, rewrite the record, and call it care.

The **Abyssal Line** explains why this method keeps working. The children taken come from the side of the line where people are rendered legally non-existent: the poor, the unmarried, the colonised, the racialised. The receiving side admits them on one condition, that they arrive without their origins.

The species-level risk lies in what this normalises. A society that accepts a paper lineage has already accepted that identity is something an institution may author on a person's behalf, without consent, and enforce for life. This publication calls the next step **The Plastic Tree**: synthetic proxies planted in the space where lineage used to be. A culture trained on amended birth certificates is well prepared to accept synthetic origins, synthetic relationships and synthetic records. Adoptees are not the edge case of that future. We are its first cohort.

The loop also transmits. Recursion means it calls itself one generation down. The adoptee who was never allowed to grieve becomes a parent carrying an unprocessed severance. The institution reads the distress it produced as evidence that more control is needed. Nothing in the circuit stops it on its own, so it has to be stopped from outside.

## The turn

All of that is the tragic reading, and this publication does not stop there.

The post-tragic position is not that adoption should be tidied up. It is that the third right, the right to parent, must be made real, and that the people the system severed must be given back what it took. Concretely, that means:

- **Fund the family, not the transfer.** Every dollar a system is willing to spend on a placement should first be available to keep the child with their mother: cash support, housing, childcare, paid leave. Poverty must never again be grounds for surrender.
- **Separate poverty from neglect** in child protection law, and repeal the timelines that terminate a family on a statutory clock.
- **Pay no one for a child.** End fee-based placement and the financial conflicts of interest of agencies that recruit pregnant women.
- **Prefer guardianship and kinship care** that give a child safety without erasing their identity, so that no child's lineage has to be cancelled for them to be cared for.
- **Build the Six Pillars**: a Royal Commission, a National Redress Scheme with lifelong trauma-informed care, an end to every record veto, adoption literacy across human-services training, an annual national acknowledgment, and birth certificates that tell the truth. Add the companion demand: no-fault, no-fee discharge of adoption orders for any adoptee who wants their original identity back.

Reproductive justice already contains the principle. The right not to have a child and the right to have one mean little without the right to raise the children you have. A movement that called itself pro-life and meant it would be fighting for that third right. It would put its money into the bond, not the pipeline.

The footnote called us supply. The work now is to make that word impossible to write again: to build a world in which no child is taken from their mother because she was poor, no record is rewritten to make the transfer look clean, and no adult has to petition the state for permission to know their own name.

That world is not built by an apology. It is built one opened record, one funded family and one repealed clock at a time. The people who were severed are already doing the work.
